# Twin rooms test exploration before memory

The E2 pilot's all-or-nothing seeds come from an exploration failure, not a memory failure. The clearest evidence is the MLP. A memoryless agent can guess a room and reach 0.5 success, the "best Markovian policy" in a two-way T-maze ([Ni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03864v4)). Instead every MLP seed sits at exactly 0, and every failed eval episode times out without entering either room. The current reward already makes a fast wrong-room guess better than wandering. So failing seeds are not trading memory for safety: they never learned to get through the shaft, corridor and junction, or to find a goal hidden 130 units behind a baffle. Seed-level success therefore measures "did this seed get lucky early" as much as "can this backbone remember", and the 1–5 of 5 recurrent spread is too noisy to rank backbones. Only LSTM-16 vs GRU (5/5 vs 1/5) is even borderline significant. Every canonical cue-recall benchmark avoids this trap the same way. The reward lands one step after the choice. Corridor progress is shaped or forced. A memoryless control reliably reaches chance. Our redesign should do the same: count success as crossing the correct door line (or remove the baffle), add cue-independent shaping for corridor progress, tighten the horizon, and keep the wrong-room terminal reward at or slightly above zero. None of these depends on the cue, so none can leak the correct room. The acceptance test for the redesign is that the MLP reaches about 0.5 on nearly every seed. Once it does, the redesign opens a new risk: the agent may carry the cue in its body pose instead of its hidden state, and that needs its own lesion test.

## Zero success below the 0.5 guessing level marks an exploration failure

The repo's reward arithmetic settles which way the agent is pushed. The twin-room env uses the base `NavigationEnv` with no distance shaping: a reward of −1/max_steps per step and +1 on success, with `max_steps = 2L + 256 = 320` at L=32 and gamma 0.999 (`envs/navigation_env.py::compute_reward`, `scripts/e2_twin_rooms.sh`). Success and wrong-room entry both terminate the episode. A timeout is bootstrapped with `gamma * V(next_obs)` from the pre-reset observation (`algorithms/ppo.py`), which is the partial-episode bootstrapping that [Pardo et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00378v2) recommend. Under bootstrapping, the critic treats a never-terminating agent as worth about **−(1/320)/(1−0.999) ≈ −3.1**. A wrong-room entry after k steps returns −k/320, which is never worse than −1. The correct room returns 1 − k/320. **Entering either room beats wandering by a wide margin, so the reward-optimal memoryless policy is a fast coin flip with about 0.5 success.** Seeds that time out on every eval episode do not optimise this reward for any memory capacity. They never found the doors.

Several mechanisms explain why a seed never finds them, and they compound. The only reward a seed sees before its first success is a constant per-step penalty. That penalty is the same wherever the agent is, so it gives no direction. A goal reward plus a small step penalty is hard for typical agents because "there is no reward signal for the initial random behaviour" ([Aquatic Navigation benchmark](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.20534)). In minimum-time sparse-reward problems, **the initial policy's goal-hit rate is a robust early predictor of whether learning succeeds** ([Vasan et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00324v2)). That fits the bimodal pattern: seeds that stumble onto the goal early lock in, and the rest never do. Gaussian PPO makes the lottery worse, because the policy "often wants to reduce the variance in selected action as training progresses", which can collapse entropy before any success ([Rahman & Xue 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.07536)). [Andrychowicz et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.05990) found that no regulariser they studied, entropy included, helped significantly. Underneath all of this sits ordinary seed variance. Ten runs of one algorithm, split into two groups of five, can produce learning curves that look like they come from different distributions ([Henderson et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.06560), as described by [Colas et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1806.08295)).

The task's own structure makes the first success unusually rare. Success needs three skills in sequence: navigating a bent shaft and an L-step corridor, making the memory-dependent choice at the junction, and then searching a 200×200 room for a radius-16 goal about 130 units behind a baffle. A failure at the third skill produces exactly the pilot's signature. Even in the correct room the agent scores 0, every episode runs out the clock, and the result lands below chance rather than at it. **Before a search policy exists, the junction choice gets almost no differential signal, because both rooms usually end in a timeout or a wrong-room termination.** The bottleneck is exploration and credit assignment, and it comes before memory.

Three repo-specific confounds could also tilt the comparison and are cheap to rule out. First, recurrent agents train at a learning rate of 3e-4 while the MLP uses 1e-4. Recurrent state also produces temporally correlated actions that carry an agent further down a corridor, so part of the "LSTM advantage" could be exploration. Second, eval uses the deterministic mean action from a zero initial state (`act_inference`). A seed whose noisy training policy reaches rooms can still have a mean action that stalls against a wall, and it would then time out on every eval episode. Logging training-episode outcomes by termination type (success, wrong room, timeout) and running a sampled-action eval separates these cases. Third, the recurrent backbones use PyTorch default init with no forget-gate bias, and one recurrent trunk feeds both actor and critic (`models/agents/recurrent_agent.py`). These points come back in the section on recurrent-PPO details.

| Candidate cause | Predicts | Cheapest check |
|---|---|---|
| Sparse reward, flat step penalty gives no direction | Bimodal seeds; early goal hits predict success | Log per-iteration junction-reached and room-entered rates |
| Gaussian std collapse before first success | log-std falls while success stays at 0 | Plot policy log-std per seed |
| Post-choice baffle search | 0 success even when the correct room is entered | Log success conditional on entering the correct room |
| Deterministic-eval artefact | Train outcomes include rooms, eval only timeouts | Eval with sampled actions; split train episodes by termination type |
| Learning-rate asymmetry (3e-4 vs 1e-4) | MLP worse for optimisation reasons | Rerun MLP at 3e-4 |
| Seed noise | Rankings flip with new seeds | Use 10+ seeds; Fisher test on solved counts |

## Every canonical memory benchmark puts the reward right after the choice

The literature designs out this confound deliberately. Bakker's original RL-LSTM T-maze was "designed to test RL-LSTM's capability to bridge long time lags, without confounding the results by making the control task difficult in other ways". A single N/S move at a perfectly signalled junction ends the episode: +4 if correct, −0.1 if wrong ([Bakker 2002](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2001/file/a38b16173474ba8b1a95bcbc30d3b8a5-Paper.pdf)). Even so, Bakker stresses that the agent "must first learn to reliably move to the T-junction". He needed a separate directed-exploration network that learned to set exploration low in the corridor and high at the junction. Ni et al.'s Passive T-Maze removes the issue entirely. The step reward is (1(x_{t+1} ≥ t) − 1)/(T−1), so the agent pays a penalty only when it falls behind the optimal rightward pace. The authors note the tasks "need little exploration because rewards are dense before the goal", and the best Markovian policy earns 0.5 ([Ni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03864v4)). In their code the passive episode length is `corridor_length + 1`, so **there are zero slack steps**. Only the time limit ends an episode, and a wrong goal simply scores 0 ([Memory-RL tmaze.py](https://github.com/twni2016/Memory-RL/blob/main/envs/tmaze.py)).

The pattern holds across benchmarks. In MiniGrid's MemoryEnv both choice objects are visible at the split, and stepping next to the wrong one ends the episode with 0 ([MiniGrid memory.py](https://github.com/Farama-Foundation/Minigrid/blob/master/minigrid/envs/memory.py)). DeepMind's Passive Visual Match is the most instructive. The agent is teleported into a small 4×7 recall room facing four coloured pads. Touching any pad ends the episode, paying **10 points for the right pad and 1 point for any wrong pad**, and no pad within 5 s gives 0 ([Hung et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.06721)). That consolation reward bootstraps the "reach the choice" skill on every episode, so memory only has to raise the payoff from about 3.25 to 10. Memory Gym holds the agent still while cues are shown and recommends checking with no-memory and frame-stack baselines. One of its variants that was thought to need memory turned out to be solvable by a 16-frame stack ([Pleines et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17207v6)). The only benchmark that hides goals, Memory Maze, measures memory as faster re-navigation over many repeated targets in fixed-length episodes, not as one-shot recall ([Memory Maze README](https://github.com/jurgisp/memory-maze)).

| Benchmark | Success | Wrong choice | Goal at choice time | Memory-length control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bakker T-maze | Correct move at junction | Ends episode, −0.1 vs +4 | Adjacent | Corridor N = 5–70 |
| Ni Passive T-Maze | At correct goal at step T | Scores 0, fixed horizon | Adjacent | Horizon = L+1, pace penalty |
| MiniGrid MemoryEnv | Tile next to matching object | Ends episode, 0 | Both objects visible | Random hallway length and start |
| TVT Visual Match | Touch matching pad | Ends episode, **+1 vs +10** | All options visible, agent faces them | Fixed phase durations via teleport |
| **Our twin rooms** | Reach hidden goal 130 units behind baffle | Ends episode, 0 | **Hidden; needs a search** | Depends on agent speed; horizon 2L+256 |

Two design lessons follow. First, our post-door search is far larger than any recall phase in the literature, and it is the stage where failing seeds die. Second, every benchmark that varies delay holds it fixed (TVT teleports, Ni sets the horizon to the corridor length) or reports it explicitly. Our horizon of 2L+256 lets a slow agent stretch its own memory requirement while losing search time. Ni et al. frame the goal formally: scale **memory length** while holding **credit-assignment length** near 1. In our task the credit-assignment path from the junction choice to the reward runs through the whole baffle search.

## Cue-independent fixes remove the lottery without leaking the room

A fix leaks the correct room only if some signal after the cue differs between the two rooms as a function of the cue. The +1 success reward already depends on the cue through which room is correct, and that is the whole point of the task. Any other term must be **symmetric across the two arms, or a function only of position along the shared shaft and corridor**. Potential-based shaping F = γΦ(s′) − Φ(s) is necessary and sufficient to preserve the optimal policy ([Ng, Harada & Russell 1999](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~shivaram/readings/b2hd-NgHR1999.html)). A potential built from geodesic progress toward the junction, clipped at the junction, therefore changes neither the optimum nor the information available. It is the continuous analogue of Ni's pace penalty.

The fixes below are ranked by how directly the literature supports them and how much of the failure they remove. The top three should go in together. Door-line success removes the search. A small wrong-room reward gives the junction a gradient. Corridor shaping gets the agent to the junction. With all three, a memoryless agent should reliably earn 0.5, and only memory can lift a backbone above that.

| Fix | Leaks room? | What it removes | Evidence and caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| Success on crossing the correct door line (or remove the baffle / enlarge goal radius) | No | Post-choice search; credit length drops to a few steps | Matches Bakker, Ni, MiniGrid, TVT. With the baffle removed, rays may see the goal from the door, which is fine because the choice is made at the door |
| Small positive wrong-room terminal reward (e.g. +0.1–0.2 vs +1) | No | All-or-nothing gradient to the junction | TVT's +1 vs +10 ([Hung et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.06721)). Shifts the memoryless optimum, so report success, not return |
| PBRS with Φ = −geodesic distance to the junction, clipped there; or Ni-style pace penalty | No | Directionless corridor phase | Policy-invariant ([Ng 1999](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~shivaram/readings/b2hd-NgHR1999.html)); Ni's pace penalty is symmetric but not strictly policy-invariant |
| Horizon = shortest path + small slack | No | Agent-dependent memory length, wasted rollouts | Ni's zero-slack design ([tmaze.py](https://github.com/twni2016/Memory-RL/blob/main/envs/tmaze.py)) |
| Curriculum over L (8 → 32 → …) using the existing `rooms/twin_rooms_L*.json` | No | Long corridor at the start of training | Memory Gym's endless tasks act as a curriculum ([Pleines et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.17207v6)). Also trains memory gradually, so apply the same schedule to every backbone and report final-L results |
| Std floor, RPO mean perturbation, init std ≈ 0.5, last policy layer scaled 100× smaller | No | Entropy collapse | [Rahman & Xue 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.07536); [Andrychowicz et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.05990) |
| Observation normalisation | No | Badly scaled ray inputs | "Always use observation normalization" ([Andrychowicz et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.05990)) |
| Episodic novelty bonus (E3B, NGU episodic term), annealed | Usually no | Corridor exploration | Episodic terms carry the benefit ([Henaff et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05805)) |
| Lifelong novelty (RND, cross-episode counts) | Indirectly harmful | — | Once the right room is learned, the wrong room becomes novel and rewarded. RND agents keep returning to rare dangerous states ([Burda et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.12894)) |
| Reverse curriculum from starts near the goal | Partly | Long horizon | [Florensa et al. 2017](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1707.05300). Starts must never skip the cue. A start past the alcove makes the correct room unobservable |
| Φ = −distance to the *correct* goal | **Yes** | — | Tells the agent the room at the junction, so memory becomes unnecessary |

The termination penalty needs care, because both directions fail. A strongly negative wrong-room penalty, worse than the timeout's −3.1 bootstrap value, makes "never enter a room" a local optimum. That would look exactly like the pilot's all-timeout seeds. Setting the wrong-room return equal to the timeout value makes the agent indifferent and removes the pull toward the doors that currently aids exploration. Diagnostic environments confirm that the sign of non-terminal rewards decides whether agents cut episodes short or drag them out ([Freire et al., DERAIL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.01365)). **Keep the wrong-room terminal at 0 or a small positive value.** On intrinsic bonuses, novelty estimates are unreliable under partial observability because "observations may look alike in different states" ([Ramesh et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.10282)). A bonus computed from a backbone's hidden state would also confound the comparison it is supposed to support. Treat bonuses as a last resort, computed from shared ray-cast embeddings and annealed to zero. Report extrinsic success only. Adding more environments or longer training is expensive. It raises each seed's odds of getting lucky, but the lottery stays.

## Closing the exploration gap opens a body-as-memory channel

Once the MLP reliably reaches the junction, the task faces a different threat: a memoryless agent can store the cue in the world. Peshkin, Meuleau and Kaelbling showed that an agent able to write bits into its own input can solve a POMDP optimally with a memoryless policy ([Peshkin et al. 1999](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0103003)). The Low-pass RNN T-maze blocks this by parking the agent in a "limbo" space for the delay. There it cannot move or change its observation, so it cannot use its position to record the cue ([Stepleton et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1805.04955)). The repo has already closed the obvious line-of-sight leak. The generator bends the shaft 90°, so the cue is out of view about two steps past the bend (`scripts/generate_twin_rooms.py`). But the corridor is 60 units wide, about 4.6 full-speed steps, and the junction is symmetric. A policy could drift to the upper or lower half of the corridor after seeing the cue, or keep a slight heading bias, and then follow that wall into the matching arm. The last speed and turning values in the `proprio` slots can carry a bit the same way. No paper documents agents learning this trick in a continuous T-maze; it is an inference from Peshkin's result and the limbo design.

This turns the redesign's acceptance test into a leak detector. **After the fixes, the MLP should sit at about 0.5. Anything clearly above 0.5 means the cue is reaching the policy through pose, proprioception or a residual ray.** The pilot already holds a puzzle that points this way. LSTM with a BPTT window of 16 solved 5/5 seeds. Yet the cue-to-decision gap is at least 9 shaft steps plus 32 corridor steps, more than 40 in all, which is far beyond the gradient horizon. Three explanations fit. The cue may persist in the forward hidden-state dynamics even though no gradient spans the gap. It may be offloaded into pose for part of the corridor. Or a residual observation leak may remain. Three tests tell them apart. A linear probe of cue identity from the hidden state and from (lateral offset, heading) at several corridor depths shows where the cue is held. A "pose lesion" re-samples lateral position, heading and last action at mid-corridor while keeping the hidden state. A "memory lesion" zeroes the hidden state while keeping pose; if success then falls to about 0.5, the cue lived in memory. An assertion that the cue-colour ray channel is zero at every pose past the bend closes the line-of-sight question for good. Design hardening follows the limbo idea: a narrower corridor, a funnel or teleport that resets lateral pose and heading at mid-corridor, and a randomised spawn pose in the alcove.

## Recurrent-PPO details can masquerade as backbone differences

Several of the pilot's backbone gaps contradict published trends, which points to implementation and initialisation rather than architecture. Ni et al. found "generally no significant difference" between LSTM and GRU in recurrent model-free RL ([Ni et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.05038v3)). Memory Gym found GRU slightly better than LSTM ([Pleines et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.11104)). Our GRU solved only 1/5 seeds. The best-documented source of early LSTM–GRU divergence is gate initialisation. **A forget-gate bias of 1 closes the LSTM–GRU gap in supervised learning** ([Jozefowicz et al. 2015](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/jozefowicz15.html)). Under PyTorch defaults the gates start near σ(0) = 0.5, and a 0.5 per-step retention erases a trace across 40 steps (0.5^40 ≈ 1e-12). Neither cell keeps the cue for free at init. A +1 forget bias for the LSTM, and the matching update-gate bias for the GRU, are cheap and backbone-fair. For the GRU, mind PyTorch's convention h′ = (1−z)·n + z·h, so the bias goes toward large z. Whether this supervised-learning result transfers to PPO is untested.

The shared actor-critic trunk is the second suspect. A shared recurrent encoder "increases the gradient norm and hinders learning", and in two simple POMDPs the shared version failed outright ([Ni et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.05038v3)). SB3's RecurrentPPO gives the critic its own LSTM by default ([SB3-contrib docs](https://sb3-contrib.readthedocs.io/en/master/modules/ppo_recurrent.html)). With value targets spanning about −3 to 1, critic gradients can dominate the shared hidden state and shape it for value prediction, which ignores the cue until memory is already in use. A separate or detached critic is a one-flag ablation.

BPTT window effects are probably noise for now. The repo stores each step's incoming hidden state and starts each window from it (`sample_mini_batch`). That is the Memory Gym/MarcoMeter scheme, so staleness is highest at window 16, the best performer. Memory Gym found recomputing stale states gave only slight gains ([Pleines et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.11104)). A small blog study found refreshing critical on a memory-heavy gridworld, with KL an order of magnitude larger without it ([Kandou](https://kandouss.github.io/blog/ppo_stale_states)). Logging approx-KL, clip fraction and gradient norm per window size settles this cheaply.

The statistics argue against reading much into the pilot. Fisher's exact test gives **p ≈ 0.048 for LSTM-16 (5/5) vs GRU (1/5)**, p ≈ 0.17 for LSTM-16 vs LSTM-64 (2/5), and p = 1 for LSTM-16 vs LSTM-256 (4/5). The window ordering 16 > 256 > 64 is not even monotonic. With only 3–10 runs, point estimates "are unlikely to provide definitive answers", and new runs can reverse rankings ([Agarwal et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.13264)). Use at least 10 seeds per backbone. Report rliable's IQM with stratified bootstrap CIs and performance profiles. If bimodality persists, report solved-seed counts with exact binomial CIs; 2/5 spans about 0.05–0.85. That follows Bakker's own "successful runs out of 10" plots ([Bakker 2002](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2001/file/a38b16173474ba8b1a95bcbc30d3b8a5-Paper.pdf)). Success conditioned on solved seeds is a fallback only, since it selects on exploration luck.

## Conclusion

The pilot's most useful number is the MLP's zero. The current task cannot tell a backbone that forgets from one that never reached the junction. The fix is not a better exploration algorithm bolted onto the same task. It is a task in which exploration stops being the bottleneck by construction, as every reference benchmark does. With the choice rewarded at the door, corridor progress shaped symmetrically and the horizon tightened, the memoryless baseline gets a precise job: 0.5 means the task works, below 0.5 means exploration still dominates, and above 0.5 means the cue is leaking through the body or the rays. The backbone comparison then becomes the gap above 0.5, sampled over L.

What stays uncertain is whether the recurrent gaps survive the redesign. None of these fixes has been tested in a continuous ray-cast T-maze, and the GRU deficit and the window-16 puzzle have plausible non-memory explanations: gate init, the shared critic, and pose offloading. The redesign should therefore ship with a forget-bias and critic-separation check, matched learning rates, and the probe-and-lesion pair. Run these before attributing any gap to memory architecture. Otherwise E2 risks publishing a cleaner measurement of the same confound.
