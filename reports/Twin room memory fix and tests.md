# Shape the corridor, then lesion the body

The best fix for E2 is one change. Add exact potential-based shaping on the **path distance to the junction's entry edge, clipped to zero at and beyond that edge**: Φ(s) = −k·d_J(s)/D_J with **k = 1**, D_J = d_J(start_pos), and F = 0.999·Φ(s′) − Φ(s). Leave the −1/max_steps step penalty, the +1 goal, the baffle, wrong-room termination and the 2L+256 horizon as they are. The L = 32 pilot locates the bottleneck exactly. Seeds that fail never reach the junction. Every seed that reaches it also finds the goal behind the baffle. Memoryless seeds that get through sit at 0.47–0.51. The earlier proposals to count success at the door line and to pay a consolation reward for the wrong room therefore target a stage that is not failing, and they should be dropped. The potential does not depend on the cue and is constant past the junction, so it cannot leak the room and does not move the optimal policy or the 0.5 memoryless ceiling. What it does is give the critic a correct value slope down the corridor, so every backbone reaches the choice. Fixing exploration brings a second problem to the surface, and the pilot already shows it. **Two of five solved LSTM-BPTT16 seeds ignore a hidden-state reset at the junction**, so their choice is committed earlier, most plausibly in lateral position or heading. That is easy in a 60-unit corridor whose upper and lower halves lead straight into the two arms. The paper therefore needs diagnostics that intervene inside the corridor: a reset sweep by position, a 2×2 hidden-state × pose swap, and a mirrored-pose push in the style of Déletang et al. It also needs a cue probe reported as gain over pose and observation baselines, because pose alone could produce the current 0.99. All of these run on saved checkpoints. Under a tight budget, about 75 extra training runs (five waves of 16) cover the controls reviewers will ask for: oracle and no-cue bounds, a recurrent control with separate trunks and a forget-gate bias, five extra seeds at the decisive L, and a cheap learning-rate check. Another 15 runs of a pose-hardened corridor are needed only if the diagnostics show that body-as-memory dominates.

## The pilot puts the bottleneck before the junction, not behind the door

The L = 32 pilot (seeds 100–104, 200 deterministic eval episodes per seed) is all-or-nothing per seed, and the dividing line is whether a seed reaches the junction (`reports/Final paper and experiment plan.md`, E2 pilot table). **No MLP seed reaches it** (one seed does so in 34% of episodes). One of five seeds gets there for each frame stack, two for LSTM-BPTT64, four for LSTM-BPTT256, one for the GRU, and all five for LSTM-BPTT16. Every seed that fails times out on all 200 episodes. **Every seed that reaches the junction also finds the goal behind the baffle.** The two memoryless seeds that get through sit at chance by always choosing one fixed room. No memoryless seed beats chance, so there is no sign of a cue leak through the rays.

This changes the diagnosis behind the earlier fix list. The earlier fixes assumed a memoryless agent might reach the correct room and still fail the 130-unit search behind the baffle, so they proposed success at the door line and a +0.1–0.2 wrong-room reward in the style of TVT's +1 vs +10 pads ([Hung et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.06721)). The pilot rules that failure out. The search succeeds whenever the junction is reached, so both changes would alter the task without touching the bottleneck. The reward arithmetic already pulls agents toward the rooms. Timeouts are bootstrapped with γV(s_T) (`algorithms/ppo.py`), which is the partial-episode bootstrapping of [Pardo et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00378v2). As a result, a never-terminating agent is worth about −(1/320)/(1−0.999) ≈ −3.1, while a wrong-room entry costs at most −1. Failing seeds lack a gradient that reaches the alcove, shaft and corridor. They do not lack an incentive. In minimum-time sparse problems, the initial policy's goal-hit rate predicts whether a run learns at all ([Vasan et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00324v2)). That matches seeds that either escape the start early or never do.

The pilot also holds two results the paper should keep. First, one LSTM-BPTT64 seed and one LSTM-BPTT256 seed reached the junction and then sat at chance. These are **genuine failures to learn the memory** at L = 32, the effect E2 exists to measure, and they appeared only once exploration stopped hiding them. Second, the junction reset gives a split result. Of the five LSTM-BPTT16 seeds, success stays at **1.00 and 1.00** for two, falls to **0.54** for one and to **0.69 and 0.72** for two. The room geometry explains why this matters. In `rooms/twin_rooms_L32.json` the corridor spans y = 270–330 and runs to a junction box at x = 506–566. The goal rooms lie directly above (y ≥ 390) and below (y ≤ 210) it. A lateral offset in the corridor therefore already points at one arm. An agent that drifts to the matching half after seeing the cue, or keeps a small heading bias, has committed before the junction. Peshkin et al. proved that an agent able to write bits into its own input can solve a POMDP with a memoryless policy ([Peshkin et al. 1999](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0103003)). Delay tasks that want internal memory park the agent in a "limbo" where it cannot move during the delay for exactly this reason ([Stepleton et al. 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1805.04955)). The pattern of resets fits a mixture of strategies across seeds: some carry the cue in pose, one reads its hidden state at the junction, and some do both.

## A junction-clipped path potential with k = 1 is the whole fix

Potential-based shaping F = γΦ(s′) − Φ(s) is necessary and sufficient for the optimal policy to stay unchanged ([Ng, Harada & Russell 1999](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pabbeel/cs287-fa09/readings/NgHaradaRussell-shaping-ICML1999.pdf)). In episodic tasks it also needs zero potential at every terminal state ([Grzes 2017](https://www.cs.kent.ac.uk/people/staff/mg483/documents/grzes17goals-in-pbrs.pdf); [Müller & Kudenko 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01307v1)). The fix is built to meet both conditions by construction. Success and wrong-room entry both happen past the junction, where Φ is defined to be zero, so no terminal state needs special handling. The specification below is complete enough to implement.

| Element | Value | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Distance d_J(s) | Geodesic path length from s to the junction box's corridor-side edge (x = 506 at L = 32), from the nav mesh (`utils/navigation_mesh.py`, Delaunay + funnel) precomputed on a grid, or arc length along the corridor centreline | Euclidean distance from the alcove and shaft points through walls, which creates the wall-hugging local optimum of distance shaping ([Trott et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.01417)); Habitat's DD-PPO shapes on geodesic distance ([Wijmans et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.00357v2)) |
| Target | The entry **segment**, not a point | In the straight corridor d_J = 506 − x, independent of lateral offset y, so shaping neither rewards nor penalises the lateral bias that could carry the cue |
| Clip | Φ = 0 inside `junction_region`, both arms and both rooms | Neither door is closer in Φ. F ≡ 0 after the junction, so the room choice and the search are credited only by the +1 |
| Normaliser D_J | d_J(start_pos), one fixed number per room JSON (≈ 13L + 230 units) | Total shaping budget = k at every L, comparable to the +1 goal, as with Ni's pace penalty bounded by 1 ([Ni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03864v4)) |
| Scale k | 1.0; fall back to 0.5 only if L = 256 seeds stall in the alcove | Stand-still stays net negative at every L (table below) |
| Discount in F | γ = 0.999, the PPO/GAE discount | Exact invariance; dropping γ (DD-PPO style) adds a small hurry bias but breaks the clean claim |
| Truncation | Use Φ(s_T) of the pre-reset state as normal; do **not** zero it at timeouts | The repo bootstraps timeouts (infinite-horizon view). Zeroing Φ there would pay +k·d_J/D_J at every timeout, most to agents that never left the alcove |
| Cue dependence | None: Φ must not read goal_pos, room identity or cue | `NavigationEnvEasy.compute_reward` shapes on distance to the goal, which sits in the cued room. That **leaks the answer**, and twin rooms must never use it |
| Everything else | Step penalty −1/max_steps, +1 success, wrong room = terminal 0, baffle kept, max_steps = 2L+256 | One change per pilot, so the acceptance test can attribute its effect |

The γ arithmetic sets k. With a negative potential that is zero at the junction, any progress earns positive shaping. Standing still earns a small positive (1−γ)·k·d_J/D_J ≤ 0.001k per step, which is the sign behaviour of negative potentials analysed by [Müller & Kudenko 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.01307v1). The table compares that with the step penalty and with the signal for full-speed progress, using D_J ≈ 13L + 230 units and the 13-unit/step maximum speed (`configs/env/base_env.yaml`). These are my own numbers. I measured the geometry on the L = 32 JSON and assumed, as the generator intends, that the other files differ only in corridor length.

| L | max_steps | Step penalty | D_J (full-speed steps) | Shaping per full-speed step, k = 1 | Net per step when standing still at spawn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 272 | 0.0037 | ≈ 26 | 0.039 | −0.0027 |
| 32 | 320 | 0.0031 | ≈ 50 | 0.020 | −0.0021 |
| 64 | 384 | 0.0026 | ≈ 82 | 0.012 | −0.0016 |
| 128 | 512 | 0.0020 | ≈ 146 | 0.0069 | −0.0010 |
| 256 | 768 | 0.0013 | ≈ 274 | 0.0037 | **−0.0003** |

Stalling stays unprofitable while k < 1/((1−γ)(2L+256)), which is 3.7 at L = 8 and 1.3 at L = 256. So k = 1 is safe everywhere, with a thin margin only at the longest corridor. Progress pays roughly 3–10 times the step penalty. The per-step signal shrinks as 1/L, like the penalty, so long corridors remain harder per step while the total budget stays fixed.

Shaping helps PPO because it gives the critic the right starting slope, not because it adds information. If the shaped critic is V′ = V − Φ, every TD residual, and so every GAE advantage at any λ, equals its unshaped value. Shaped PPO is unshaped PPO whose critic starts offset by Φ. This is the actor-critic counterpart of Wiewiora's result that potential-based shaping equals Q-initialisation from Φ ([Wiewiora 2003](https://www.jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/10338)), and of the observation that shaping "just adds baseline to estimator" ([Berkeley Deep RL lec. 6](https://rll.berkeley.edu/deeprlcoursesp17/docs/lec6.pdf)). The derivation is mine. Under sparse reward the early critic is roughly constant, so corridor advantages are noise until a first success. That is the pilot's lottery. Under shaping the early critic already encodes "closer to the junction is better". Three consequences follow for measurement. Asymptotic success and the 0.5 memoryless ceiling should not change. Sample-efficiency metrics such as AUC now describe a shaped task and must say so. The existing unshaped L = 32 pilot doubles as the free unshaped reference row. Faster, straighter traversal also tightens the cue-to-decision delay. In this geometry the cue leaves view about 30 units past the bend, so the correlation horizon ξ is close to **L steps at full speed** (`memory_horizon_steps: 32` in the JSON). Logging the realised steps from last cue sighting to junction entry turns ξ into a measured quantity rather than a nominal one. Cherepanov et al. warn that variable horizons hide long-term-memory failures ([Cherepanov et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06531)), which is the reason to fix and report ξ.

Implementation needs care at three points. The shaped reward at the last step of a truncated episode must use Φ of the pre-reset observation, the same one the bootstrap already uses. The stored previous potential must be reset with every autoreset. Finally, a unit test next to `test_twin_rooms_cue_hidden_past_the_bend` should assert that Φ is identical for both cue values and zero everywhere in the junction box and both rooms. That test is the formal leak check. MIKASA-Robo's RememberColor shows why it matters. Its dense reward pulls the gripper toward the cube of the cued colour, so the shaping itself encodes the answer ([MIKASA-Robo `remember_color.py`](https://github.com/CognitiveAISystems/MIKASA-Robo/blob/main/mikasa_robo_suite/rl/memory_envs/remember_color.py)). The paper can cite it as the contrast case.

Three cheaper alternatives are worse. A Ni-style pace penalty does not leak, but it is not exactly policy-invariant, and with a turn-rate-limited agent the "optimal pace" around the bend must be guessed. A curriculum over L using the existing `rooms/twin_rooms_L*.json` does not leak either, but backbones that learn faster advance sooner and so train on different distributions, and it tests delay extrapolation rather than learning at L. It is the right fallback only if shaped runs still fail at L ≥ 128. Start-position curricula past the bend skip the cue or make the delay variable, which is the mixed-ξ setup Cherepanov warns against. Removing the bend would let the 180° rays re-read the cue. The policy's state-independent log-std starts at 0 (std 1), and the clamp LOG_STD_MIN = −5 (`models/heads/policy_head.py`) gives no effective floor. Std is therefore a possible secondary cause. A floor near log 0.2 is worth adding only if the logged log-std of failed pilot seeds collapsed before their first junction visit, since no studied regulariser reliably helped PPO in [Andrychowicz et al. 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.05990).

The acceptance test on a new shaped L = 32 pilot (the same 35 runs, which then count as the sweep's L = 32 cell) has three parts. Every seed of every backbone must reach the junction in nearly all eval episodes. Every memoryless seed must sit inside the 200-episode binomial band around 0.5, roughly 0.43–0.57. No memoryless seed may sit above that band. A memoryless seed above chance would mean that the cue now reaches the policy through pose, proprioception or a ray.

## Moving the lesion into the corridor separates memory from the body

The current diagnostics cannot separate memory from the body. The probe in `scripts/e2_diagnostics.py` already splits by episode, which is correct. Its 0.99 accuracy at every distance bin is still uninformative. Once the trajectory depends on the cue, every later ray cast and pose depends on the cue too, so a probe on the hidden state may only be re-reading the agent's position. The junction reset comes after the commitment point it was meant to find. Déletang et al. state the problem directly: whether an agent uses internal memory or off-loads it onto the environment is "undetectable without intervening" ([Déletang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.03938)). Their floor-memory T-maze is almost the E2 setup in a gridworld. The agent sees a cue, walks a wide corridor, and near its end is pushed to the opposite wall. The two agents look identical before the push, with about 0.98–0.99 correct for both. After it the LSTM stays at **0.996**, while a memoryless CNN that had stored the cue in its lateral position drops to **0.107 and 0.004**.

The diagnostics below make that intervention, and its counterpart on the hidden state, the core of the E2 table. Each prediction column says what a seed would show if the cue lived only in the hidden state (internal) or only in pose (body).

| Diagnostic (eval only, saved checkpoints) | Internal memory predicts | Body-as-memory predicts | Source of procedure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reset sweep: zero (h, c) once at the bend exit, corridor 25/50/75/100%, junction | ≈ 0.5 for every reset point | Full success for resets after the commitment point; the curve's step locates it | Position-indexed version of Wijmans' memory budget ([Wijmans et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.13261v1)) |
| Hidden-state swap: paired cue-A/cue-B episodes from one RNG seed; copy (h, c) from B into A at mid-corridor, keep A's pose | Choice follows the donor cue (B) | Choice follows the physical cue (A) | Activation patching; no published RL precedent found (own suggestion) |
| Pose swap / mirrored push: keep h, set y → 600 − y about the centreline, mirror heading and the last-turn proprio slot | Unchanged | Falls below 0.5 | [Déletang et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.03938) |
| Autopilot: scripted centreline driving through the corridor at policy speed, observations still fed to the RNN, control returned at the junction | Unchanged | ≈ 0.5 | Own suggestion |
| Memory-budget truncation: rebuild h_t from zeros over the last k observations, k ∈ {1, 4, 16, 32, 64, ξ} | Falls to 0.5 once k < ξ | Barely changes | [Wijmans et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.13261v1) |
| Probe gain: accuracy of a probe on [pose; o_t; h] minus one on [pose; o_t], per distance bin | Large | ≈ 0 | Control-task logic of [Hewitt & Liang 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03368) |

The 2×2 design (hidden state from A or B × pose from A or B) is the decisive test, because it separates the two channels within a single seed. Two controls keep it honest. Patched states can be off-distribution, so a behaviour change from patching alone does not prove a mechanism ([Taufeeque et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15421v2)). One control therefore swaps in the hidden state from a *same-cue* episode at the same corridor position. That swap should not change success, and if it does, the drop is damage from patching rather than memory. The other control matches the mirrored push with a random lateral and heading kick of the same size. If any perturbation breaks the agent, the push result says nothing about memory. Junction-reach rate and success given that the junction was reached must be reported under every intervention, so that a navigation breakdown after a reset is not read as forgetting. Déletang ran 1,000 Monte-Carlo rollouts per condition. That also suits a per-seed binomial estimate here: the 95% half-width at p = 0.5 shrinks from about ±0.07 at the current 200 episodes to about ±0.03 at 1,000.

The probe should follow the protocol Wijmans et al. used for their collision and map decoders ([Wijmans et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.13261v1)), with these additions. Probe h, c and [h; c] separately, because the LSTM cell state is the natural long-term store and h mixes in the current observation. Fit the same L2-regularised logistic probe to five baselines in the same bins: the current observation, pose (y, heading, speed), [o_t; pose], an untrained network's states along the same trajectories, and a shuffled-label control. Use episode-grouped cross-validation. Refit the probe within lateral-offset strata, for example only steps within ±5 units of the centreline. If cue accuracy collapses once y is held fixed, the probe was reading pose. An L1-sparse probe finds the cue units for an ablation. Following [Taufeeque et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.15421v2), editing the hidden state along the probe direction then shows whether the decodable cue is actually used. Their Sokoban agent's actions followed such edits. The headline number is the **conditional gain of h over pose and observation**, not raw accuracy. POPGym Arcade's gradient-based recall density is an optional complement that shows which past observations the policy actually draws on ([POPGym Arcade](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.01450v6)).

Each test is reported **per seed**, and each solved seed is classified as internal, body or mixed. The pilot already shows a mixture, and a pooled mean would average it away. Each backbone then gets three horizon numbers. The first is the largest fixed L it solves ([Ni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03864v4) swept their T-maze from 50 to 1,500). The second is the truncation lag k at which success reaches chance. The third is the earliest corridor position after which a mirrored push stops hurting, which is the commitment point. Cherepanov's framework says what these numbers can support. Frame stacks have a hard context of 4 or 16 steps. The LSTM's BPTT window is a gradient horizon, not a context limit. "LSTM-16 solves ξ ≈ 32" is therefore a claim about memory beyond the gradient window only if the reset sweep shows the cue living in the hidden state across the corridor ([Cherepanov et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06531)). If most solved seeds turn out to commit through pose, that is a reportable finding in its own right, for the paper's perceive/remember framing as well. But then E2's nominal L overstates the memory demand, and the conditional pose-hardened run below becomes necessary.

## About 75 runs buy the controls reviewers will ask for

The order of work follows from cost. All of the diagnostics above, plus four further eval-only checks, run on checkpoints that the shaped sweep produces anyway. The first further check reports deterministic and sampled-action eval side by side: the best memoryless POMDP policy is generally stochastic ([Montúfar et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.07206)), and the pilot's memoryless seeds reached 0.5 only by always picking one room. The second masks or flips the cue at test time. The third trains at L and tests at L/2 and 2L, which is Cherepanov's recommended way to expose failures that in-distribution success hides. The fourth logs per-loss gradient norms on the shared trunk. This eval battery comes first because its outcome sets L*, the largest L at which the best recurrent agent still succeeds. L* and the next level up, L*+, are where the paid runs go: "spend runs where curves transition". Run costs come from the E0 measurements: about 2.1 h for a full MLP run and 2.8 h for an LSTM run, and 0.2–0.5 h when a solved run stops early (`reports/Final paper and experiment plan.md`). One wave is 16 concurrent jobs.

| 1 | No-cue LSTM-64 (both cue colours identical) | 2 L | 10 | That the shaping, geometry or timing leaks the room to an agent *with* memory; the memoryless MLP at 0.5 covers only reactive leaks |

The ordering reflects what the shaped task changes. The oracle comes first because H2 predicts recurrent failure at large L. Without an agent that is handed the cue, the paper cannot say whether L = 256 fails on memory or on navigation, and the oracle is the cheapest run class in the table. The no-cue control is needed even though the MLP already sits at chance, because a recurrent agent could exploit a leak that a reactive one cannot, such as elapsed time. The recurrent best-practice control ranks above more seeds because the most cited failure mode for recurrent actor-critic is exactly the repo's design: one recurrent trunk feeding both heads (`models/agents/recurrent_agent.py`). Value targets span roughly −3 to 1, which favours critic-dominated gradients. One combined configuration is cheaper than a 2×2 factorial. If it matches the shared-trunk results, the shared trunk is reported as harmless. If it is better, it becomes the headline and the shared trunk becomes the ablation. The learning-rate check ranks last because shaping caps the memoryless rows at 0.5. Their learning rate can change how fast they reach the junction and their AUC, but it cannot change the success-vs-L headline. On the recurrent side, the E4 pilot already found 1e-4 the worst rate at every BPTT window, though on medium_maze rather than twin rooms. Of the swap, only the MLP and FS16 at 3e-4 is still informative. FS16 matters most at L = 8, where its 16-frame context spans ξ.

The statistics show why priority 3 is not optional. With five seeds, the exact 95% Clopper–Pearson interval for 5/5 solved is [0.48, 1.0] and for 3/5 is [0.15, 0.95]. Ten seeds narrow 10/10 to [0.69, 1.0], and a 10/10 vs 0/10 split then has non-overlapping intervals. The pilot's sharpest backbone gap, LSTM-16 at 5/5 vs GRU at 1/5, reaches only Fisher p ≈ 0.048. With 3–10 runs, point estimates "are unlikely to provide definitive answers" ([Agarwal et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264)). The paper should report the fraction of seeds solved with exact binomial intervals next to rliable's IQM with stratified-bootstrap CIs, plus the probability of improvement for the key pairs. Environment and agent seeds should be kept paired across architectures, as Patterson et al. recommend.

Second-tier runs answer reviewer complaints that a workshop paper can defer with a sentence. Four cue values across four rooms (+30 runs, already optional in the plan) answer the "it's only one bit" objection raised in the reviews of Ni et al. ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=APGXBNkt6h)) and lower chance to 0.25. A distractor phase between cue and junction (+30) tests robustness to noise, which the same reviewers asked for. Variable-L training (+15) has to be read against the fixed-L results, because mixed horizons hide failures. Hidden-state refresh or burn-in for LSTM-16 at L*+ (+5–10) tests stale stored states. Memory Gym found the effect slight ([Pleines et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17207)). A transformer or SSM row is an upper reference, not an expected baseline at L ≤ 256. The literature is mixed: GRU was the best general model in [POPGym](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01859), and transformers pull ahead of LSTMs only from memory lengths around 250 upward in [Ni et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03864). The paper should also state that POPGym found plain MLPs winning most navigation tasks. That makes the measured memoryless floor at every L a required figure element, not decoration.

## Conclusion

The pilot shifts what the paper must prove. It does not need to prove that the twin rooms require memory: memoryless agents already sit at chance once they reach the choice. It needs to prove where the memory lives. The shaping fix is almost free of design risk, because it is invariant, cue-blind and zero wherever the episode can end. It still changes the experiment's meaning: once every seed reaches the junction, success above 0.5 can come from the hidden state or from the body, and the current junction reset and raw probe cannot tell them apart. The intervention that settles the question is Déletang's corridor push combined with a hidden-state swap. Neither needs a single new training run.

The deeper implication is that L is a nominal memory demand until the diagnostics measure the commitment point. If recurrent agents hold the cue internally only until they can steer into the matching half of the corridor, the real memory horizon is a few steps at every L. H2's predicted failure horizons would then show up as navigation-plus-commitment effects rather than memory limits. Running the eval battery on the shaped L = 32 pilot before launching the remaining 140 sweep runs is therefore the highest-value step. It decides whether E2 measures the hidden state as intended or needs the pose-hardened corridor first.
